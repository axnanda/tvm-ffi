## 1. What arXiv 2602.00545 claims

*Depth, Not Data: An Analysis of Hessian Spectral Bifurcation* (Shenyang Deng, Boyao Liao, Zhuoli Ouyang, Tianyu Pang, Yaoqing Yang) was submitted 31 Jan 2026 (v1) with an HTML rendering stamped v3, 24 May 2026 [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00545]. Author pages list it as accepted at ISIT 2026 with "Full Version, Code, Blog" links whose targets were not recoverable [T, https://sites.google.com/site/yangyaoqingcmu/academic-projects]. v1 and v3 differ at least in the step-size constant and reference numbering, so v3 is the version to cite [T, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00545v3].

The abstract argues that the "bulk-and-spike" spectrum, previously attributed "to the imbalance in the data covariance matrix", "can arise purely from the network architecture, independent of data imbalance": "even when the data covariance is perfectly balanced, the Hessian still exhibits a Bifurcation eigenvalue structure: a dominant cluster and a bulk cluster", and "the ratio between dominant and bulk eigenvalues scales linearly with the network depth" [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00545].

Pith's four major objections, reconstructed from snippets [T, https://pith.science/paper/2602.00545]: (i) Lemma A.9 "silently assumes the input covariance is effectively rank r and that singular values beyond r are zero at time t", and Lemma A.8 plus the Weyl step depend on that decay; (ii) "V is only column-orthonormal; its first r rows are not generally orthonormal when d_0 > d*"; (iii) "the proof covers only the case d* = d_0 ≤ d_L"; (iv) the two-cluster result "depends on the target being aligned with the singular vectors of the random initialization", so the "independent of data imbalance" claim "is stronger than what the construction proves". Pith proposes the control "train on perfectly whitened data with a random target cross-covariance that is independent of the initialization" and calls nonlinear extension "a testable open question, not an established result".

Inferences [I]: the intervals in C3 are disjoint iff (m_t+δ_t)^{2(L−1)} < L(m_t−δ_t)^{2(L−1)}, i.e. exactly the spread condition in C7; because the aligned rank-r target has unit singular values, population GD should drive δ_t → 0, so the ratio converges to L late in training and Corollary 3.6 is the t → ∞ limit of Theorem 3.5. For the paper's L = 3 setting (p = 920) the predicted zero count is 920 − 16 − 72 = 832.

## 2. Prior theory: deep linear networks, bulk-and-spike, Gauss–Newton

**Dynamics and balancedness.** Saxe, McClelland & Ganguli showed deep linear nets "have nonlinear gradient descent dynamics on weights that change with the addition of each new hidden layer" and recommended orthogonal initialization [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120]; their per-mode decoupling under whitened input is what Initialization 2.1 reproduces [I]. Arora, Cohen & Hazan derived end-to-end dynamics with "no dependence on layer widths" [T, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/arora18a.html]. Du, Hu & Lee proved that gradient flow "effectively enforces the differences between squared norms across different layers to remain invariant", with discrete GD preserving balance only approximately [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.00900]. Arora, Cohen, Golowich & Hu obtain linear-rate convergence when hidden widths are "at least the minimum of the input and output dimensions" and weights are "approximately balanced", warning that at large depth Gaussian init may have no standard deviation "small enough to ensure approximate balancedness" — hence an explicit balanced initialization [P, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.02281].

**The H_o + H_f decomposition.** The naming comes from Singh, Bachmann & Hofmann: "we will refer to the first term H_o as the outer-product Hessian, while we coin the second-term H_f as the functional Hessian"; for deep linear nets the Hessian rank "effectively depends on the sum of hidden-layer widths", and the "linear rank formulas faithfully capture the numerical rank for … rectified and hyperbolic tangent networks" [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.16225].

**The data-side account the paper argues against.** Sagun et al.: the number of outliers "is often equal to the number of classes C"; "increasing parameters mainly scales the bulk, while changing the data … mainly affects the outliers" [T, https://leon.bottou.org/publications/pdf/tr-expl-2017.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.04454]. Papyan's class/cross-class structure "underlies many visually striking features in deep network spectra" [P, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.11865]; in a deep-UFM experiment with K = 4, "removing the cross-class component eliminates exactly K(K−1) = 12 outlier eigenvalues, while removing the class component eliminates K = 4" [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06106]. Sankar et al.: the layer-wise outlier count "is also C" [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03801]. Ghorbani, Krishnan & Xiao: without batch norm "large isolated eigenvalues appear rapidly"; "in batch normalized networks, these two effects are almost absent" [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.10159]. Pennington & Worah's random-matrix Fisher analysis finds "linear networks are worse conditioned than nonlinear ones" [T, https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/18bb68e2b38e4a8ce7cf4f6b2625768c-Paper.pdf]. Cohen et al.: under GD "the maximum eigenvalue of the training loss Hessian hovers just above 2/(step size)" [P, https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/poster/2577].

**Concurrent competitors.** Singh, Monzio Compagnoni & Orvieto derive closed-form eigenvalues "for linear networks of arbitrary width and depth" and find MSE-classification sharpness "tracks the share of samples in the largest class" under a Gauss–Newton, isotropic-feature analysis [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13631]. Zhang et al. argue "C is a primary driver of the near-block-diagonal Hessian structure" [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02809]. Arjevani attributes clusters and outliers to symmetry breaking from "a nearby, highly symmetric reference configuration", including in the Gauss–Newton matrix [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09919].

Key inference [I]: Papyan's C + C(C−1) = C² Gauss–Newton outliers coincide numerically with r² when r = C (one-hot MSE targets). Counting alone cannot separate "depth" from "class structure"; the discriminating prediction is the linear-in-L ratio with a depth-constant bulk.

## 3. What is known about SNN loss landscapes and Hessians

**There is no loss whose Hessian this is.** Gygax & Zenke show surrogate gradients (SGs) "cannot be understood as gradients of a surrogate loss": the closed-loop integral of the SG field is non-zero, "not explained by numerical integration errors", and the SG sign sometimes differs from the exact gradient so "SG descent is not guaranteed to reach a local minimum of the loss" [P, https://zenkelab.org/2024/04/elucidating-the-theoretical-underpinnings-of-surrogate-gradient-learning-in-spiking-neural-networks/ ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14964]. The 2019 "virtual loss" framing it refutes already noted the true spiking loss "displays characteristic plateaus with zero gradient" [P, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.09948]. Eilers et al.'s SG-NTK pairs the standard Jacobian with a surrogate quasi-Jacobian (Θ_SG = J J̃ᵀ) [T, https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/10f1737aa6347ccc555ac068e1b45523-Paper-Conference.pdf]. Hence the Jacobian of the SG update field is generally non-symmetric, may have complex eigenvalues, and the Weyl step of Section 1 does not transfer [I].

**Exact-gradient landscapes are piecewise.** EventProp gradients match finite differences to relative deviation below 1e-7, but "are ignorant with respect to these critical points and are therefore unable to selectively recruit additional spikes or dismiss existing spikes" [P, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-91786-z]. Wenig, Memmesheimer & Klos: with LIF "arbitrarily small parameter changes can make spikes appear or disappear"; "for LIF, the sample loss splits into regions separated by jumps. For QIF … boundaries are continuous" (QIF: 90.1% SHD) [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03935]; exact QIF gradient descent reaches 97.3% MNIST [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14523].

**Measured curvature is sparse and mostly proxy.** Lui & Neftci computed layer-wise Hessian traces by Hutchinson through SG-BPTT; quantization loss "correlated well with each layer's Hessian trace" (0.3% accuracy loss at 58% size reduction) [P, https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3477145.3477158]. IKUN reports PyHessian top-50 eigenvalues for a 2-layer conv SNN ("close to 0 and positive"); Pith: "the proof is missing and the experiments are too thin" [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18250]. Zhang, Hu & Min attribute "an exceptional large Hessian spectral radius" to "repeated inputs inherent in direct encoding and the gradient accumulation characteristic of BPTT" and reduce ρ(H) by deflating dominant gradient directions [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11134]. A2SG claims SG-trained deep SNNs "converge to sharper regions of the loss landscapes than DNNs" and that for window width β < 1 "the Hessian magnitude is scaled up by 1/β²"; evidence is a Conv1 2D slice and a Fisher proxy because "direct Hessian computations are intractable"; SAM gives only 94.84% → 94.87% on VGG16/CIFAR-10 [T, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11236v1 ; https://pith.science/paper/2606.11236]. SAST is the only explicit smoothness bound: the surrogate-forward objective "is β-smooth, with β depending on depth, temporal gain, and surrogate slope" [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09696]. Zenke & Vogels: SG learning "is robust to different shapes of underlying surrogate derivatives, but the choice of the derivative's scale can substantially affect learning performance" [P, https://direct.mit.edu/neco/article/33/4/899/97482].

**Depth effects.** Direct training was limited to "<10 layers" before tdBN enabled 50 [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05280]; 34-layer Spiking ResNet underperforms 18-layer [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.04159]; "the gradient explosion/vanishing problem in SNNs is severer over ANNs" [P, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19725]; "a large proportion of spiking neurons fall outside the gradient-available interval … especially as the layer deepens" [P, https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0335.pdf]; "the intrinsic gradient error is magnified through layerwise backpropagation" [P, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/deng23d.html]. Rossbroich et al.: Kaiming "failed in CSNNs with seven or more hidden layers"; fluctuation-driven init with σ_U = 1 works, σ_U = 2 inflates deep-layer rates, σ_U = 20 saturates [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10226].

**Temporal axis.** SLTT finds temporal backprop "contributes little to the final gradients" [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14311]; DeepTAGE finds membrane distributions "increasingly deviate from the firing threshold as time progresses" [P, https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/6b6492cd06db22bac024506e9ed0925e-Paper-Conference.pdf]. Flatness claims in TET, TRT and IVON rest on 1D/2D slices, sometimes inconsistent across datasets [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.11946 ; https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19256 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08624].

Bottom line [I]: no 2019–2026 paper reports SNN Hessian eigenvalues versus L or T, cluster counts, or an H_o/H_f split. Every claim C1–C11 is untested for SNNs.

## 4. Methodological consequences for testing the claims on SNNs

1. **Define "the Hessian" explicitly and report more than one.** Primary: the symmetric Hessian of a *surrogate-forward* smooth loss (SAST-style, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09696), a true loss that GD minimizes. Secondary: the symmetric Gauss–Newton proxy J̃ᵀJ̃, the SNN analogue of H_o and of the A2SG Fisher proxy and SpiKFAX's Kronecker-factored Fisher (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29379v1). Diagnostic: the Jacobian of the SG update field, reporting complex eigenvalues and ‖J − Jᵀ‖/‖J‖ as a direct non-conservativeness measure (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14964). Optional surrogate-free arm: QIF neurons with exact gradients (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03935).
2. **Decompose.** Compute H_o and H_f separately and track ‖H_f‖/‖H_o‖ against the loss; C6 predicts exponential decay, and the non-conservativeness results give reason to expect failure [I].
3. **Fix the surrogate slope/scale.** A2SG's 1/β² rescaling and Zenke–Vogels' scale sensitivity mean β must be held constant across depths; separately test whether the dominant and bulk clusters scale identically in β.
4. **Treat T as a second depth.** Run a joint L × T grid; DSD predicts ρ(H) grows with T under direct (repeated) encoding, SLTT predicts weak T-dependence for static inputs; compare direct and Poisson encoding.
5. **Control threshold and spike rates.** Use fluctuation-driven init (σ_U = 1) so L ≥ 7 trains; log per-layer firing rate and the fraction of units inside the SG window (Lian et al.) to separate curvature from SG sparsity.
6. **Remove known outlier suppressors/creators.** No BN/tdBN (Ghorbani), no residual shortcuts (SEW), bias-free layers so the multiplicity formulas apply.
7. **Match the theorem's data and initialization.** Whitened Σ_xx = I; zero-padded I_r target with r ≤ d_0 ≤ d_L (Pith's proved regime); arms: (a) balanced-SVD init aligned with the target, (b) USI, (c) Kaiming, (d) randomly rotated target, (e) full-rank tail. Full-batch GD at constant η < min{1/L, 1/M^{2L−2}}, monitoring λ_max against 2/η for edge-of-stability clamping.
8. **Metrics.** Counts (r², (d*+d_L−2r)·r, remaining zeros), ratio of mean dominant to mean bulk eigenvalue over training and across L = 2…7, bulk constancy, and the Papyan C²-vs-r² check with a C-class variant.
9. **Linear control arm.** Replicate 10→20→20→16, r = 4 exactly (expect 16 / 72 / 832, ratio ≈ L), then tanh, then SNN.

## 5. Open questions and gaps

- Exact definitions of m_t, δ_t, α and M in Theorem 3.5, and which figure holds the tanh L = 2–7 sweep, were never surfaced verbatim [https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00545v3]; whether the tanh gap ratios are numerically ≈ L or merely monotone; whether biases are included in p.
- Official code/blog URLs, the ISIT 2026 program entry, and the v2 date/changelog.
- Depth-dependence of sharpness beyond the EoS clamp (Marion–Chizat; Mulayoff–Michaeli), Jacot–Gabriel–Hongler, Pennington–Bahri, Liao–Mahoney, and arXiv 2604.11639 / 2602.07135: not retrieved (truncated set; search budget exhausted).
- Which SNN "Hessian" (if any) shows an r² cluster; whether the effective depth is L, T or L·T; whether β rescales clusters uniformly.
- Whether H_f decays under SG training given sign mismatch and a piecewise-constant exact loss; whether bounded SG derivatives make the bulk shrink geometrically with L instead of staying constant.
- Whether any realistic spike encoding satisfies the rank-r / zero-tail premise, or synthetic whitened rank-r spike inputs must be built.
- What A2SG and SpiKFAX (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29379v1, which asserts a "sharp loss landscape" without reporting a spectrum) actually measured for "sharpness"; the closed form of SAST's β-smoothness constant in depth and temporal gain.
