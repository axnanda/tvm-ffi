## 1. HTSR theory and what alpha means

**Phases.** HTSR identifies "5+1 Phases of Training" (Random-like, Bleeding-out, Bulk+Spikes, Bulk-decay, Heavy-Tailed, Rank-collapse) "corresponding to increasing amounts of Implicit Self-Regularization"; a small model traverses all of them "simply by changing the batch size" [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01075]. Rank-collapse is "marked by a large-mass spike at λ=0" and framed as over-regularization [T, https://jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-410/20-410.pdf]. The weightwatcher.ai phase table marks alpha as "unstable"/not meaningful in Random-like, Bleeding-out and Bulk+Spikes, gives 4 ≤ α ≤ 6 for Bulk-decay/weakly heavy-tailed, 2 ≤ α ≤ 4 (ideal ≈ 2) for Heavy-Tailed, and a separate "Ideal" row at α ≈ 2 with the ERG condition satisfied [T, https://weightwatcher.ai/htsr.html]. Hence [I] alpha is interpretable only after a layer leaves Bulk+Spikes; SNN dead units (mass at λ=0) and the rank-one feature-mean spike mimic Rank-collapse and Bulk+Spikes respectively.

**Universality classes.** The classes are defined on the tail index µ of the matrix *elements*: "0 < µ < 2: VHT ... 2 < µ < 4: MHT ... 4 < µ: ... vanilla MP behavior in the infinite limit"; "µ = 2 and µ = 4 are corner cases that we don't expect to be able to resolve at finite N" [P, https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~mmahoney/pubs/sdm20-ht-accuracies.pdf]. WeightWatcher maps the fitted ESD exponent to µ piecewise: µ = 2(α−1) for α < 2, µ = 2 for 2 ≤ α ≤ 3, µ = α−1 for α > 3 [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/weightwatcher/RMT_Util.py]. The Mahoney group's own 2025 HTMP model notes "while eigenvalues of weight matrices in modern state-of-the-art models are heavy-tailed, elements are not" [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03470], so a "VHT class" claim from alpha alone is an operational mapping, not a measured element tail [I].

**Operational bands.** "The best models have layer alphas that lie between 2 and 6"; "when comparing models, smaller <alpha> is better"; alphas below 2 are "rare and suggesting over-training"; smaller alpha is better "but only up to a point" [T, https://weightwatcher.ai/]. The README: "models with smaller PL exponents alpha, on average, correspond to models that generalize better" and best layers have "PL exponents alpha closer to 2.0" [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/README.md]. The 2026 grokking paper: "the lower bound α=2 ... is a hard cutoff, whereas the upper bound α≳5–6 is looser because it can depend on the aspect ratio Q" [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02859]. The warning strings are hard-coded thresholds 2.0 and 6.0 [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/weightwatcher/constants.py].

**alpha = 2 as the RG / ERG condition.** SETOL derives the HTSR metrics via an Effective Hamiltonian that "closely parallels ... the Wilson Exact Renormalization Group"; the ERG condition is det(Ã) = 1, i.e. "Tr[ln Ã] = ln det(Ã) = 0" on the tail; "the HTSR conditions for ideal learning (α=2) align with the independent SETOL condition"; "when these two conditions align, we propose the NN layer is in the Ideal state" [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17912]. α = 2 "resembles in spirit an ERG Universal Critical Exponent at a phase boundary between the heavy-tailed and very heavy-tailed phases"; α < 2 implies "unusually large variance and an atypical weight matrix", "possibly slightly overfit, with the potential for hysteresis-like memory effects"; for α ≥ 2 "the generalizing eigen-components will mostly concentrate into the tail ... but some will remain in the bulk"; and "when one layer is undertrained (α>6) other layers may become overtrained to compensate (α<2)" [T, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17912]. SETOL's own validation is a 3-layer MNIST MLP where "the train-test gap tends to shrink as α falls toward 2 from above" and a tail-only "smoothed" FC1 reproduces test error [T, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17912]. The Dec-2024 post calls this "equivalent to taking 1 step of the Wilson Exact Renormalization Group" for layers "trained to optimality" [T, https://calculatedcontent.com/2024/12/24/weightwatcher-htsr-theory-and-the-renormalization-group/]. Reading [I]: α ≈ 2 is where the tail transformation is volume-preserving, a steady-state condition; it is an alignment of two conditions, not a proof that α = 2 maximizes accuracy, so "critical ≠ optimal" is our critique of the author's framing.

**alpha-hat and failure modes.** α̂ = α·log10 λ_max "weighs bigger layers more" [T, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-24025-8]; it originates as a "weighted average of PL exponents" validated on 50+ pre-trained ImageNet models, not small MLPs [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08278]. Two documented failures: "Scale Collapse", where λ_max shrinks (pruned/distilled models) and breaks α̂ while plain α "behaves as expected", and a Simpson's paradox in which pooled average-α-vs-quality correlations flip sign across tasks [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00734].

**Alpha keeps falling after accuracy stops improving.** README: "as the training and test losses decrease, so does alpha. But when the test loss saturates and then starts to increase, alpha drops below 2.0"; the early-stopping rule "only work[s] for very well trained models, where the optimal alpha=2.0 is obtained" [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/README.md]. In the MNIST double-descent demo "at the point of peak test accuracy, all layer α's are close to ... α ≈ 2" and then "drop significantly below 2" [T, https://www.weightwatcher.ai/dd.html]; in grokking, average α goes 4.0 → 2.9 → 1.1 while accuracy rises then collapses [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02859]. Forcing α → 2 with WW-PGD on a 3-layer Fashion-MNIST MLP is "roughly neutral at this scale" (98.05 ± 0.13 vs 97.99 ± 0.17; implausibly high for Fashion-MNIST) [T, https://calculatedcontent.com/2025/12/13/ww-pgd-projected-gradient-descent-optimizer/]. An independent Bayesian derivation finds the marginal likelihood "convex in a heaviness parameter" with "an optimal heaviness" [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13123]. The literature splits between alpha-as-diagnostic (warnings, early stopping) and alpha-as-objective (WW-PGD; TempBalance [T, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.00359]); no verbatim "diagnostic, not an objective" quote was found [I].

## 2. Running and reading WeightWatcher correctly

**Version.** master is 0.8.8, PyPI ships 0.7.7; install from git and record the version [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/weightwatcher/__init__.py].

**Real defaults.** `analyze()` defaults: min_evals=8, max_evals=15000, max_N=50000, plot=False, randomize=False, mp_fit=False, detX=False, vectors=False, fix_fingers=False, fit='PL', pl_package='ww', xmax=None, pool=True [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/weightwatcher/weightwatcher.py; constants in https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/weightwatcher/constants.py]. The README says "the power law fits need at least 50 eigenvalues to make sense" while docstrings say 10 and 0; the code value (8) wins, so pass min_evals=50 explicitly [P, README URL above].

**What gets analyzed.** Types DENSE/CONV1D/CONV2D/EMBEDDING; NORM/UNKNOWN skipped; a layer is skipped if M < min_evals or N > max_N. For 2-D weights N, M = max/min of the shape and the ESD is the M squared singular values, so 784×256 gives 256 eigenvalues, 256×10 gives 10, a table (V, d) gives d regardless of V [P, weightwatcher.py]. PyTorch detection is `isinstance(nn.Linear)` or 'linear'/'embedding' in the class name, reading `.weight.data`; biases ignored; `layer_id` counts every module from `named_modules()`, so run `describe()` first. Passing `model.state_dict()` analyzes every 2-D tensor regardless of owning class, the cleanest route for custom LIF or lookup modules [P, weightwatcher.py].

**The fit.** Default fitter WWFit: for every candidate xmin, continuous Clauset-Shalizi-Newman MLE α = 1 + n/(Σ log x_i − n log xmin), KS distance D, pick xmin minimizing D; sigma = (α−1)/√n; eigenvalues ≤ 1e-5 dropped; xmax=None so the top eigenvalues are never clipped by default [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/weightwatcher/WW_powerlaw.py]. Below SMALL_N_CUTOFF = 20 eigenvalues a different bias-corrected estimator (k_min=8) is used, so a 10-eigenvalue head is fit by a non-comparable heuristic [P, same URL]. The estimator "overestimates small alpha<1.5 and underestimates large alpha>3.5" [T, https://calculatedcontent.com/2018/09/09/power-laws-in-deep-learning/]. The author's LoRA bands transfer directly: rank ≥ 64 "alpha is reliable", rank < 32 "alpha can be off by a noticeable margin" [T, https://calculatedcontent.com/2024/01/27/evaluating-fine-tuned-llms-with-weightwatcher-part-ii-peft-lora-models/]; a 1×100 matrix produced NaN fits in issue #40 [T, https://github.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/issues/40].

**Columns and gotchas.** Power-law columns: alpha, xmin, xmax, D, sigma, num_pl_spikes (tail length used), status, warning; TPL adds Lambda; clip_xmax adds raw_alpha, num_fingers [P, weightwatcher.py]. A failed fit sets alpha = −1 and is then labelled 'over-trained'; `get_summary()` is a plain column mean including such rows, so filter `status=='success' & 0<alpha<8` first [I, weightwatcher.py]. The README discards α > 8 and warns that for multi-scale tails "sometimes alpha only fits the very last part of the tail" [P, README]. `fix_fingers='clip_xmax'` refits after dropping up to max_fingers=10 top eigenvalues until α drops by finger_thresh=1.0, stopping at α ≤ 2 [P, RMT_Util.py]; the option is "advanced" "because the impact hasn't been studied in detail" [T, https://calculatedcontent.com/2023/03/21/weightwatcher-advanced-features-fix_fingers/]. `xmin_peak` / `E_TPL` require pl_package='powerlaw' [P/I, weightwatcher.py]. With TPL, "Lambda ... now useful when the (TPL) alpha < 2.0 ... smaller Lambda implies better generalization" [P, README].

**Scale, MP and random metrics.** mp_fit=True rescales so Σλ = N, grid-fits σ_MP, sets bulk_max = (σ(1+1/√Q))², counts num_spikes above bulk_max + √TW, and mp_softrank = bulk_max/λ_max [P, weightwatcher.py]. randomize=True element-permutes W (5 replicas if n_comp < 100) and reports max_rand_eval, rand_distance, ww_softrank, ww_maxdist, rand_num_spikes (README: num_rand_spikes) [P, weightwatcher.py]; rand_distance is Jensen-Shannon on the two *sorted eigenvalue arrays*, not histograms, and is noisy for small M [I, RMT_Util.py]. A correlation trap is a spike in the *randomized* ESD; such layers "have not been trained properly because they look almost random" [P, README]; the 0.8 trap-burden tools "were vibe coded and are not yet extensively tested" [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/WeightWatcher/master/docs_trap_features.md]. detX=True rescales and finds the largest tail with product ≥ 1, giving detX_num, detX_val, ERG_gap = detX_num − num_pl_spikes; ideal layers have α ≈ 2 with the detX and xmin lines overlapping [P, RMT_Util.py; T, https://weightwatcher.ai/setol.html]. vectors=True adds tail/bulk eigenvector entropy and localization columns [P, weightwatcher.py]. plot=True writes esd1-5 per layer; esd4 (D vs xmin) and esd5 (α vs xmin) show whether α rides a finger [P, weightwatcher.py].

**Embeddings and lookup tables.** EMBEDDING is analyzed as the table matrix (N = vocabulary, M = d); tables with N > 50000 are silently skipped unless max_N is raised [P, weightwatcher.py]. No source says what HTSR phases mean for a sparsely updated table [I].

## 3. What the L3 architecture is

"L³: Large Lookup Layers" is Tseng & De Sa (Cornell), arXiv 2601.21461, ICML 2026 poster [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21461]. It "generalizes embedding tables to model decoder layers", using "static token-based routing to aggregate a set of learned embeddings per token in a context-dependent way", plus "an information-theoretic embedding allocation algorithm"; models up to 2.6B active parameters "strongly outperform both dense models and iso-sparse MoEs" [P, same URL]. A layer "takes as input a hidden state x∈ℝ^(d_in) and corresponding token ID t"; "the token ID t is used to select embeddings K_t, V_t. Embeddings are aggregated with attention from the token hidden state x"; K_t is (d_t, d_in), V_t is (d_t, d_emb), with d_t embeddings allocated to token t; the layer also has W_up (d_emb → d_up), a LayerNorm, and W_mix ∈ ℝ^(d_out × (d_in + d_up)) [P, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21461v1]. Forward: softmax(K_t x) over only token t's rows, aggregate V_t, up-project, LayerNorm, concatenate with the residual and apply W_mix [T, https://pith.science/paper/2601.21461]. The paper's Appendix A.4.4 gives a minimal PyTorch implementation plus a FlexAttention variant [P, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21461].

The user's repo is that appendix code with seven fixes (slicing, norm_out dimension, LZW mapping, flex-attention indexing, undefined names) [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/axnanda/l3-large-lookup-layers/main/README.md]. `class L3(h, n_emb, d_emb, d_up)` holds `w_k = nn.Linear(h, n_emb, bias=False)` (weight (n_emb, h) = stacked key table), `w_v = nn.Linear(d_emb, n_emb, bias=False)` (stacked value table), `w_up`, `w_mix = nn.Linear(d_up + h, h, bias=False)`, two RMSNorms; tokens are sorted, scores masked with `torch.where(seq_sort == last_token, score, -inf)`, softmaxed, and the per-token slices are `[bounds[t]:bounds[t+1]]` of the tables [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/axnanda/l3-large-lookup-layers/main/l3_layer.py]. The only discrete operation is the gather; attention is differentiable, so no STE is needed [I].

Uncertainties [I]: the allocation rule (`train_lzw` suggests LZW compression counts; the derivation was unreadable); initialization; the d_t = 1 case (trivial softmax); benchmarks seen only via abstract. Fashion-MNIST has no token IDs, so any adaptation must invent a routing key (quantized patch, hash, or k-means code), an untested departure from the paper.

## 4. Heavy-tailed weight statistics in spiking, STE-trained and embedding layers

No source reporting HTSR alphas for SNN layers was found (budget exhausted before SNN-specific queries) [I]; what follows is predictive.

**Rank-one feature-mean spike.** Our `docs/theory_notes.md` shows that for non-negative spike-rate features Σ_a = µµᵀ + Cov_a has a single dominant direction, and that the SNN update-field Jacobian is block upper-triangular with readout block I ⊗ Σ_a, so its spectrum is d_out copies of eig(Σ_a) plus zeros [I, file docs/theory_notes.md §3, §5]. Since each update is ΔW ∝ δ aᵀ, W accumulates a common-mode component along µ, so (i) WᵀW gains one outlier that, with xmax=None, can drag the KS-chosen xmin to the top and fit α on a few eigenvalues (small num_pl_spikes, large D), and (ii) the nonzero element mean survives permutation, so the randomized ESD has a spike and the layer is counted as a correlation trap even when benign [I, weightwatcher.py; RMT_Util.py]. This is exactly the shape of the JMLR Bulk+Spikes phase in which alpha is "unstable" [T, https://weightwatcher.ai/htsr.html].

**Dead units and depth.** "A large proportion of spiking neurons fall outside the gradient-available interval ... especially as the layer deepens" [P, https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0335.pdf]; Kaiming init "failed in CSNNs with seven or more hidden layers" while fluctuation-driven init with σ_U = 1 works [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10226]. Rows that never receive gradient stay at init, so the ESD mixes an MP-like bulk with a trained tail; exact-zero rows are dropped (1e-5 threshold) and counted in weak_rank_loss, pushing layers toward Rank-collapse and below min_evals [I, weightwatcher.py; constants.py].

**Noise from threshold crossings.** Surrogate gradients "cannot be understood as gradients of a surrogate loss" and "SG descent is not guaranteed to reach a local minimum" [P, https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14964]; the SG scale "can substantially affect learning performance" [P, https://direct.mit.edu/neco/article/33/4/899/97482]; for window width β < 1 curvature "is scaled up by 1/β²" [T, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11236v1]. HTSR attributes phase progression to SGD noise (batch size) [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01075], and the HTMP line models heavy eigenvalue tails without heavy elements [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03470]. Inference [I]: threshold-crossing noise acts as near-isotropic noise on W, inflating σ_MP and bulk_max, lowering num_spikes and pushing α upward; the SG scale β rescales the update field and can cause Scale Collapse in α̂ without changing α. HTSR's self-regularization presumes SGD on a loss; an SG field has no potential, so the mechanism need not carry over.

**Lookup / embedding tables.** Only the d_t rows of tokens present in a batch receive gradient, so row norms follow token frequency rather than learned cross-row correlations; the d eigenvalues of a (V, d) table then have a tail set by frequency heterogeneity, which permutation destroys, so rand_distance is large and α may be small for reasons unrelated to generalization [I, weightwatcher.py]. W_up and W_mix are ordinary dense layers and the right place to read α [I].

## 5. Recommendations for the experiment

1. **Widths.** Hidden widths ≥ 128 (256-512 preferred) so every analyzed layer has ≥ 64 eigenvalues, the author's "reliable" band [T, LoRA post URL above]; pass `min_evals=50` [P, README]; exclude the 10-way readout from averages. For L3 use h, d_emb ≥ 128 so w_k.weight and w_v.weight yield ≥ 128 eigenvalues; keep n_emb ≤ 50000 or raise max_N [P, weightwatcher.py].
2. **Call pattern.** `describe()` first; then `analyze(min_evals=50, plot=True, savefig=<run dir>, randomize=True, mp_fit=True, detX=True, vectors=True)`; a second pass with `fix_fingers='clip_xmax'`; a third with `fit='TPL', pl_package='powerlaw'` for layers with α < 2 [P, weightwatcher.py; README]. Use `state_dict()` for custom LIF/lookup modules; record `inspect.signature(analyze)` and the version [I].
3. **Columns to log per layer per checkpoint.** N, M, Q, num_evals, alpha, raw_alpha, num_fingers, xmin, D, sigma, num_pl_spikes, status, warning, Lambda, alpha_weighted, log_spectral_norm, log_norm, stable_rank, entropy, num_spikes, mp_softrank, rand_distance, rand_num_spikes, ww_softrank, ww_maxdist, detX_num, ERG_gap, weak_rank_loss, tail eigenvector entropy [P, weightwatcher.py]; filter `status=='success' & 0<alpha<8` before averaging [I].
4. **Controls for SNN artefacts.** (a) Center hidden rates before the readout and re-run WW; the feature-mean spike should collapse [I, theory_notes §3]. (b) Report num_fingers and raw_alpha minus clipped alpha. (c) Count dead units directly against weak_rank_loss. (d) Train a shuffled-label control on the same non-negative inputs so element-mean traps are separable from learned structure. (e) Hold the SG slope β fixed across arms because of the 1/β² scaling [T, A2SG URL above].
5. **Lookup-table handling.** Analyze the stacked K and V tables as EMBEDDING matrices and, separately, W_up/W_mix; add a frequency-matched null (permute rows within token groups) and report per-token-block row norms [I]. Apply the "critical α ≈ 2" reading to W_up/W_mix only.
6. **Runs and protocol.** Follow the author's 2026 baseline: fixed train/validation split, test set "monitoring-only", WW at initialization and every N updates [P, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CalculatedContent/rg_optimizers/main/README.md]; ≥ 5 seeds per arm [T, WW-PGD URL]; 35-100 epochs so the α-through-2 crossing is observable [T, https://www.weightwatcher.ai/dd.html]; arms MLP3, L3-ANN, L3-SNN at ≥ 2 batch sizes, since batch size alone moves phases [T, https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.01075]. Compare equal-depth arms with average alpha, unequal-depth arms with alpha_weighted/log_alpha_norm [P, README]. Run SETOL's tail-only smoothing test on layers reporting α ≈ 2 [T, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17912].
